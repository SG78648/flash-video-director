# FlashAcq, the plan to get a breakout audience

Written 2026-10-19 from scratch, ignoring everything that came before. Instagram was read logged out, so only what it shows without an account.

## 1. What the numbers say

Three things follow from that.

1. **Their audience came from three outliers, not from steady posting.** Nine recent reels sit at 11K to 32K and three old ones did 1.7M to 3.1M. Virality is a power law. So the plan is to take as many well built shots at an outlier as possible, and to have the account ready to turn one outlier into followers.
2. **The reel that did 3.1M (The Roll-Up Strategy) has 127.4K likes and 480 comments, and most of the comments are one word, "Guide".** It is a worked example with a shocking ratio (about $1.05M of cash becomes a $24M business), original audio, and a one word comment trigger. Nothing else.
3. **Their bio does the selling.** It names who the account is for (professionals), what they get ($1M+ businesses) and the one action (DM a word). FlashAcq's bio names what the owner does, not what the viewer gets.

## 2. What Instagram rewards

From several third party write-ups, so treat the details as secondhand. They agree on the same short list.

- **Watch time**, meaning completion and replays. A short reel watched to the end and again beats a long one watched once.
- **Sends per reach**, meaning how many viewers send it to someone in a DM. It is described as the strongest signal for reaching people who do not follow you yet.
- **Likes per reach**, a ratio, so a small audience that loves it beats a big one that shrugs.
- **Trial Reels** show a reel to non-followers first and keep it off your grid and your followers' feeds. The window is about 72 hours and Instagram has not published what counts as a pass. The follower minimum is reported as 200 for professional accounts by some sources and 1,000 by others, so check in the app.

What this means for every reel we make. It has to hold a stranger to the last second, and it has to give them a reason to send it to one specific person.

## 3. The audience to take

The first two scripts assumed a viewer who already cares about real estate. Almost nobody the recommendation engine shows a new reel to does, so they would swipe before the hook lands. Guildshore reached 230K people interested in buying businesses, and that audience had to come from somewhere. The cold audience Instagram tests a new reel on is everyone, and everyone includes **people who pay rent**.

So every reel starts from the viewer's own money, not from the industry.

- Who the viewer is. Anyone who pays rent, plans to, or has someone close to them who does. Tens of millions of people, almost none of whom follow a real estate account.
- What they care about. Their rent, why it went up, where it goes and whether they will ever own something. That is a personal, slightly angry topic, and anger and surprise are what get a reel sent to someone.
- The hook shape. "You" and a number, about something the viewer already pays for. Your landlord, your rent, your deposit, your lease.
- The turn. The reel shows how the other side makes money from the viewer (the exposé), says plainly what the other side also pays for (so it is fair and does not read as a rant), and ends with what people who understand the formula do. That is the bridge from a curious renter to a future owner.
- The conversion. A keyword that offers the how-to, so the people who care raise their hand and everyone else still gave the reel its watch time and sends.

Narrow real estate reels (the lender and cap rate scripts) still have a place, but only as a second tier for people the first tier has warmed up.

## 4. Fix the account in one day

- **Name field** (it is searchable) Flash | Landlord Secrets.
- **Bio** Showing renters how landlords really make money, so you can too. DM KEYS for the one page guide. Then the link.
- **Link** one page with the free sheets (below) and a short form for people who want to partner. Today's link goes to a personal page.
- **Profile photo** the bolt logo, already made.
- **Pinned posts** the three best reels once there are three worth pinning.
- **Keyword funnel** each reel asks for one word in the comments (KEYS and RENT for the broad reels, DSCR and CAP for the narrow ones). A comment triggers an automatic DM with the matching sheet. Use a Meta approved tool (ManyChat is one) and read Instagram's current automation rules before switching it on. The comments tell Instagram the reel is worth showing and the DMs build a list you own.

The sheets must exist before the first reel that offers them.

- **KEYS guide, one page** how a first buyer lets tenants pay the mortgage (a small building, or a house with a unit that can be rented), with one worked example and the questions to ask before buying. Check the loan rules and numbers with real lenders before sending it.
- **RENT guide, one page** how to see what an apartment building is worth (income divided by the cap rate), what the numbers mean for a tenant and what a renter can do with that knowledge.
- **DSCR checklist** what the lender checks (the rent against the payment, credit, down payment, cash reserves), the usual range of each, the trade offs (a higher rate, a bigger down payment, a prepayment penalty), and the questions to ask a lender. Verify every figure with real lenders before sending it.
- **Cap rate sheet** the formula, the table of one building at 5, 6, 7 and 8 percent, and the three ways an owner can raise income.
- **Deal checklist** the numbers to run on any building before an offer.

## 5. The content engine

**Six formats, in this order of priority**

0. **The exposé of how the other side makes money from the viewer.** Landlord, bank, deposit, lease, rent increase. The first two scripts of the plan are this. It is the broadest hook there is, since it starts from what the viewer pays, and it is true, so it survives a hostile comment section.
1. **The worked example with a shocking ratio.** The guildshore hit shape. The hook is the result as a number, the body walks the arithmetic in round numbers, the close is a rule.
2. **Contradiction plus arithmetic.** The hook says the opposite of what the viewer believes, with a number, and the body proves it. This is the dissonance hook and the two scripts below use it.
3. **News reaction.** The Fed meeting, a mortgage rate move or a big headline, turned into one number on the same day. News reels get searched.
4. **Listing teardown.** A public listing, the real numbers, a verdict in one line.
5. **Reply reels.** Every good comment or question becomes the next reel, with the commenter's question on screen.

**Rules for every reel**

- 25 to 40 seconds. The two new scripts are 35 and 43.
- The hook is on screen from the first frame (the Flash and Mint styles already do this).
- One idea, one number the viewer can do in their head, no jargon without a one line meaning.
- The ending hands back to the hook so the reel loops and gets replayed.
- The call to action sits where the value peaks and names a person, for example "someone who thinks they do not earn enough to buy", never just "a friend".
- The first line of the caption is the keyword offer. Pin a comment that asks a numbers question. Reply to every comment in the first hour.
- Original audio for explainers. A trending sound is for reach reels and is a separate test.

**Volume and testing**

- Four weeks, two reels a day, 56 reels. Scripts are written in batches of 14 a week.
- Each idea gets three hook variants (only the first line changes). If Trial Reels are available, run them there first and publish the winner. If not, post the variants a few hours apart and keep the one with the best three second hold and sends per reach.
- Working targets (my judgment, not Instagram's numbers). Kill a hook that loses most viewers in the first three seconds. Keep a format only if sends per reach rises over a week. A send for every 100 views is strong.
- When any reel passes 100K views, post three follow ups within 24 hours (part two, a reply to the top comment, and a "what I would do") and pin the original.

## 6. Distribution beyond the algorithm

1. **Cross post natively** the same file, with no watermark, to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels and LinkedIn video. That is four more shots a reel. LinkedIn is the closest thing to guildshore's audience, since it is full of the professionals we want.
2. **Collabs.** A list of 30 real estate and small business creators with 5K to 50K followers. The offer is specific, "I turned the numbers in your last post into a reel, want to publish it as a Collab". Aim for five collabs in 30 days. A Collab post shows on both audiences.
3. **Comments on bigger accounts.** Twenty useful comments a day on posts in the niche in the first half hour after they go up, each with a number or a fact. No links, no pitches, and nothing that reads as spam.
4. **Story replies and DMs** answered by hand the same day. These are the first people who send reels on.
5. **Paid boost** about $300 in the first month, spent on the reels with the best organic signals, aimed at professionals 28 to 45 who like real estate investing. The aim is to buy data on hooks faster and to put a good reel in front of the right people. Boosted views are legitimate and the budget is the only risk.
6. **A face.** One reel in four with the owner on camera for ten seconds at the start or the end. Trust is the thing a faceless account lacks, and guildshore's results come with borrowed authority (named operators and firms). Never invent authority.
7. **Email list** from the keyword funnel, since it is the only audience that does not depend on Instagram's mood.

## 7. What I would not do

Buying followers or likes, comment pods, bots, mass follow and unfollow, fake screenshots or invented results, scraping other people's data. They get the account limited or removed, which ends the plan, and a fake audience does not send reels to anyone.

## 8. The weekly scoreboard

Per reel at 48 hours the three second hold, the average share of the reel watched, sends per reach, follows per 1,000 views and keyword comments. Every Monday the top three formats get doubled and the bottom three are dropped. Judge at day 30. If 56 well made reels never pass 50K views, the problem is the hook or the format and not the account, so change those and run another 30 days.

## 9. Honest expectation

Nobody can promise a breakout. What this plan does is raise the number of real attempts to about 56 in a month, make each one built for completion and sends, test hooks before betting on them, give a breakout somewhere to land (a bio and a keyword that convert) and add shots through other platforms and collaborations. That is the only reliable way to be around when an outlier happens.

## Numbers to check before posting

20
- A $240,000 loan at 7 percent over 30 years has a payment of $1,596.73 for principal and interest, and $1,597 x 360 = $574,920, so "about $575,000". $300,000 x 1.03 to the power of 30 = $728,179, so "about $728,000" if the house grows 3 percent a year. Growth is an assumption and can be zero or negative.
- The tenant pays rent and the landlord pays the loan, but the rent also has to cover taxes, insurance and repairs and some months it does not cover everything. The script says so ("he covers the taxes and repairs"). It is a simplification of where the money goes, so keep the caption fair and never call landlords thieves.

21
- $100 x 12 = $1,200. $1,200 / 0.06 = $20,000. $20,000 x 50 = $1,000,000. 1 / 0.06 = 16.7, so "about $16" is rounded down.
- This is how income producing apartment buildings are priced. A single house for rent is usually priced by comparable sales and not by its rent, so the claim is about apartment buildings (the script says so). Cap rates differ a lot by place and building, and a rent increase is not always collected.

18
- $500,000 x 25 percent = $125,000. A $375,000 loan at 7 percent over 30 years has a payment of $2,495 for principal and interest. With about $700 a month of taxes and insurance that is $3,195, which is "about $3,200". $4,000 / $3,200 = 1.25 (it is 1.252 with the exact payment).
- The rent, the rate, the taxes and the insurance are illustrations. Lenders differ in the minimum ratio (often 1.0 to 1.25), the down payment (often 20 to 25 percent), the reserves they want and the prepayment penalty. DSCR lenders still check credit and cash reserves. Say "some lenders", as the script does, and check current terms.

19
- $300,000 / 6 percent = $5,000,000. $300,000 / 7 percent = $4,285,714, which is $714,286 less. $300,000 / 5 percent = $6,000,000, so five to seven percent is a $1.71M swing and six to five is $1M.
- A cap rate does not move one for one with interest rates and the building's income can change too. The script treats the cap rate as the number buyers will pay, which is the simple version.
