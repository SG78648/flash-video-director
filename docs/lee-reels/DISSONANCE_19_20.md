# Lee reels 19 and 20, hooks built on cognitive dissonance

Both scripts open with a sentence that two things the viewer believes cannot both be true, and neither resolves until the last third. The answer is the reason to stay. Every fact comes from Lee's own public LinkedIn posts, so nothing here is invented. They are written as flowing narration, as the other scripts now are.

| | 19 The renovation trap | 20 The confident operator |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | The best upgrade in an apartment building costs almost nothing, and most owners never make it. | The more confident an operator sounds about returns, the less you should trust them. |
| Belief it hits | Upgrades cost money and money buys value. | Confidence is a sign of competence. |
| The clash | An upgrade that costs almost nothing beats an expensive one, so what is it. | Confidence lowers trust, so what are they hiding. |
| The resolution (held to the last third) | Answering maintenance requests fast, so residents stay a year longer. | Serious investors move carefully, and trustworthy operators spend more time on what could go wrong. |
| The receipt | $150 x 12 = $1,800 a year, and $15,000 / $1,800 = 8.3 years to pay back. | The three red flags as ticked rows (projected returns, vague debt, a 47 slide deck). |
| Ask | Send this to someone who thinks fresh paint makes a building valuable | Send this to someone who's about to invest with an operator |
| Spoken words | about 120 | about 99 |

## Where each fact comes from (check each with Lee)

19, from his posts on value-add and on apartment owners losing money.
- A $15,000 unit renovation that raises rent $150 a month, compared with keeping residents an average of one extra year.
- A maintenance request that takes 8 days can make a tenant consider moving, and across hundreds of tenants this quietly costs a building millions.
- The $3,200 a unit turnover figure is from his 72 unit analysis, so the script says "in the market I looked at". Keep that wording.
- "Can come out ahead" is my reading of his comparison. He should confirm he would say it that way.

20, from his post on how to lose an LP's trust.
- Leading with projected returns over track record, being vague about the debt (a "standard senior loan"), a 47 slide deck sent cold with no earlier conversation.
- His closing point that serious LPs move carefully and trustworthy operators explain what could go wrong. His post lists two more steps (calling every risk manageable, aggressive follow up) that are not in the reel, so the reel says three, not five.

## Packaging

### 19
- **Cover text** $15,000 kitchen. 8 years to pay back.
- **Caption**
  ```
  Send this to someone who thinks fresh paint makes a building valuable.
  A $15,000 renovation that adds $150 a month takes more than 8 years to pay back. Keeping a resident one more year can beat it.
  Illustrative numbers from one market. Costs vary. Educational only, not advice.
  #multifamily #apartmentinvesting #valueadd #commercialrealestate
  ```
- **First comment** What is the slowest maintenance request you ever waited on? I'll tell you what it likely cost.

### 20
- **Cover text** Confident operator. Red flag.
- **Caption**
  ```
  Send this to someone who's about to invest with an operator.
  Lead with projected returns, stay vague about the debt, send a 47 slide deck cold. That is how operators lose serious investors.
  Educational only, not investment advice.
  #passiveinvesting #syndication #commercialrealestate #lpinvesting
  ```
- **First comment** What is the first thing you ask an operator? Tell me and I'll tell you what I ask.

## Note on 20 and securities rules

It is aimed at people who might invest with an operator. It teaches how to judge one and does not offer an investment. Keep it that way, and have his securities lawyer look at any call to action that moves from teaching to inviting investors.
