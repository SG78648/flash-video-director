Before you buy any apartment building, ask for three papers, because the seller's summary is the least useful page.
[hook icon receipt]
[broll documents | contract]
First is the rent roll, which lists every unit, what it should pay, when the lease ends and who is behind.
[broll bills | invoice]
Second is the trailing 12 months of income and expenses, which shows what actually came in, not what should have.
[broll apartment | hallway]
Say a 20 unit building shows $1,200 a unit on the rent roll.
[$1,200 x 20 units x 12 = $288,000 a year]
[broll laptop | notes]
But the last 12 months show only $251,000 collected.
[$288,000 - $251,000 = $37,000 a year]
[broll clock | thinking]
That gap is vacancy, free months and tenants who never paid, and at a 7% cap rate it is worth about half a million dollars of price.
[$37,000 / 7% = $528,571]
[broll repairs | keys]
Third are the real utility and tax bills, because taxes can reset after you buy and a seller's number won't include that.
[broll calm | residential aerial]
So price the building on what came in, not on what the rent roll says it should.
