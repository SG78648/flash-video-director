Before you buy a building, ask one question, what happens if 10% of the units go empty.
[hook icon warning]
[broll apartment | storm]
Say a building brings in $250,000 a year and costs $120,000 to run, which leaves $130,000 of income, and the loan payment is $100,000.
[$250,000 - $120,000 = $130,000 a year]
[broll laptop work | notes]
That covers the loan 1.3 times, and most lenders want at least 1.25, so on paper it passes.
[$130,000 / $100,000 = 1.3]
[broll rain window | stress]
Now lose 10% of the rent, which is $25,000, and the income drops to $105,000.
[$130,000 - $25,000 = $105,000]
[broll clock | thinking]
That covers the loan only 1.05 times, so one bad winter leaves you almost nothing, and the building didn't change at all.
[$105,000 / $100,000 = 1.05]
[broll calm | city aerial]
So I stress test every deal before I make an offer, and I walk away when it only works in a perfect year.
