# Lee Gunasinghe, the Rage-Truth playbook

A template for getting Lee seen, built on the Roy Lee (Cluely) idea that conflict is distribution, with the part that sank Cluely's credibility taken out. It sits on top of `GROWTH_V3_HARDBALL.md` and `LINKEDIN_DATA_AND_PLAN_V4.md`.

## 1. What Roy Lee actually did, and what to copy

From public reporting (secondary sources, so the quotes are best checked against the original talks).
- Cluely was launched on the tagline "cheat on everything", chosen to start arguments. Roy Lee had already gone viral as a Columbia student who documented using an AI tool in Big Tech interviews and got into trouble for it.
- He told TechCrunch Disrupt 2025 that outside deep tech a company has to focus on distribution, that going viral even when it makes people angry is the strategy that matters in the attention economy, and that founders have to be extreme, authentic and personal.
- The company raised $15 million from Andreessen Horowitz after a provocative claim spread. That claim was later disputed.
- In March 2026 he posted a retraction. The $7 million of annual recurring revenue he had given a reporter the summer before was false, and his own Stripe figures showed about $2.7 million consumer plus $2.5 million enterprise as of June 2025. TechCrunch's reading is that attention alone does not build a durable business.

**Copy.** A face and a voice that are personal, a position that makes one group argue, a high posting rhythm, and a story people can retell in one sentence.
**Do not copy.** Any claim that cannot survive a skeptic. Lee's whole business is trust (private lenders and partners write cheques on it), so he cannot afford a single number that is not real. The Cluely lesson is that the anger gets you in the door and a false number gets you thrown out.

## 2. The translation, "Rage-Truth"

The enemy is a bad practice, not a person. The hero is the math. The proof is a real deal. Lee does not need an invented villain, because his own posts already contain strong ones.
- Projections sold as if they were facts ("your underwriting model is lying to you").
- Value-add deals that are really floating rate bets (the 72 unit deal he walked away from).
- Hustle culture that does not tell you the downside (the heart attack with six flips running).
- The idea that you need a bank (the $42,000 a year seller note).
- Excitement sold as the product, against predictability ("the most boring investor you will meet").

People argue with these, so they watch to the end, comment and send. And every one of them is true, because it comes from his own public posts.

## 3. The template

About 35 to 45 seconds. The engine already builds every beat from the script, so the structure is in how the lines are written.

| Time | Job | How it is built |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 1.5 s | **Accusation or dare**, with a number | The whole hook is on screen at frame 1 and readable on mute. Examples, "Your underwriting model is lying to you." "I walked away from a $4.1 million deal." |
| 1.5 to 6 s | **The receipt** | One real fact or figure, big. The new stat scene draws figures like 89% occupied or 18% below market. |
| 6 to 25 s | **Escalation in threes** | First, Second, Third cards, or a calculation that builds. Each one is worse than the last. Say "the third is the one everyone misses" at about 8 s to hold viewers through the middle. |
| 25 to 32 s | **The verdict** | One quotable line, his rule. "A deal that only works if everything goes right is a bet." |
| 32 to 40 s | **The dare** | A question that splits the audience (BUY or WALK), then a send ask. The closing card repeats it. |

Edit rules for the render.
- A new picture every 2 to 3 seconds, never a talking head or a held title.
- Numbers count up and carry a sound (the Lee fast pace does this, and it stays a per project choice).
- No intro, no logo, no "hey guys".
- The last frame hands back to the hook so the reel loops.
- Captions on and the key number in the accent colour.

## 4. Series, because a series gives people a reason to come back

1. **Buy or Walk.** Day one is a real deal with only the numbers (reel 15). Day two is the reveal and the three reasons (reel 16). The comment prompt is BUY or WALK. The reveal turns every commenter into a returning viewer. One a week.
2. **Boring Investor Rules.** One rule from his 16 years per reel (reels 17 and 18 are the first two).
3. **Receipts.** Real numbers from closed deals (reel 14, the seller note).
4. **Kitchen to 600 doors.** The story reels (12 and 13).
5. **Wednesday roast.** Clips from the Circle talk, with consent, where he scores a deal in 30 seconds.

## 7. The ethical and legal line, written down

1. Every number comes from his own deals or is labelled an illustration.
2. Opinions are opinions, and facts are facts. Nothing implies a result.
3. No person, firm or competitor is named or described in a way that could be defamatory.
4. No invented comments, DMs or reactions. Quote only real ones, with permission.
5. No promised returns. No "invest with me" in public. If he raises money privately, his securities lawyer should read the call to action first, because public advertising of an offering has rules.
6. Clips of the Wednesday talk need attendee consent.
7. His cloned voice is used only for his own words and with his say so. Better still, his own voice on the three story reels.

## 8. How to measure it

Per reel, in Insights. Average watch time as a share of length (aim above 50 percent), the 3 second hold, sends, and non follower share of views. Review after seven reels. Keep the shape that wins and drop the weakest. The rule so far is that a scene plus a number beats a general lesson, and that has held on both @lilanthagunasinghe and @flashacq.
