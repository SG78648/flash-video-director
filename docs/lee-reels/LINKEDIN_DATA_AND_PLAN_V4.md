## 2. What it says

1. **He started posting for real in January and it worked a little.** Before that the profile got about 50 impressions a month. Since then it gets 1,000 to 5,000.
2. **The best five days of the year were a cluster of personal story posts, 21 to 25 March.** "In 2020 I had a heart attack while managing...", "I arrived in the US with $527...", "Last month I walked away from a 72 unit deal", "I am probably the most boring investor you...", and "How to lose an LP's trust in 5 steps". Together they made 4,447 impressions and 85 engagements, and March became the best month with 153 new followers. Stories in a burst beat one post at a time.
3. **Personal stories get the most engagement for their reach.** The $527 post got 22 engagements on 695 impressions (3.2 percent) and the heart attack post got 20 on 920 (2.2 percent). Generic lesson posts ("16 years in real estate reduced to 5 lessons", "If you're reading this") got 2 engagements on 230 to 270 impressions.
4. **The biggest single reach (1,900) was a position, not a story.** "I am probably the most boring investor you will meet" says he is deliberately conservative and sells predictability. A clear, slightly contrarian identity travels.
5. **Followers do not follow reach.** July had 347 impressions and 101 new followers, May had 2,764 and 188. So most new followers are not coming from his posts. Likeliest sources are the Wednesday Zoom, connection requests, comments he leaves on other people's posts and referrals. This is a guess, so ask him where people say they found him.
6. **His audience is peers and capital, not beginners.** Agents, brokers, owners and founders in New York. That suits what his posts are written for (passive investors and private lenders), and it is the wrong audience for "help 9 to 5ers own commercial real estate". LinkedIn is where his credibility and his capital sit. Instagram, YouTube Shorts and TikTok are where beginners are. They are two separate jobs.
7. **Almost all reactions come from the same circle** of about ten people who comment on everything. That is a loyal base and a ceiling. Growth needs strangers.

## 3. What it changes in the plan

**He has real proof, and the reels have not used any of it.** Public facts from his own posts.
- He arrived in the US with $527, washed dishes, cooked, managed restaurants, and learned construction on weekends.
- Today he is at 600 doors of commercial real estate.
- In 2020 he had a heart attack with six flips running (four crews, three open permits, one buyer under contract). His wife took power of attorney and his attorney handled the closings. He sold every house and moved to commercial.
- He closed a 128 unit deal with a $1.05 million seller note at 3.5 percent, where a bank wanted 7.5. That is $42,000 a year in interest.
- He walked away from a 72 unit deal ($4.1 million, 89 percent occupied, floating rate debt, turnover at $3,200 a unit).
- 16 years in real estate and 15 years running kitchens in Manhattan.

This is the strongest content a faceless account can have, because the voice can be his and the numbers are real. The earlier plan treated him as a generic teacher of math. He is a chef turned operator with a near death story, and that is what people share.

So v4 changes four things.
1. **Retire generic example reels** (the $450 lot rent examples) in favour of reels built from his own deals and story. Keep a few as filler.
2. **Post stories in bursts** of three to five in one week, then deals and math the week after, the way March worked.
3. **Use LinkedIn for the same stories, in text and native video**, and Instagram, Shorts and TikTok for the beginner version of the same stories.
4. **Put the Zoom first.** It is his most likely follower source. Make the reels send people there with a time that works (an evening session, and a replay).

Checks for Lee before posting

- 13 ends "That's not a business. That's a job with extra risk." That is an editorial line, not his wording. Keep it or change it.

Order to post them. 12 first (the widest audience), then 13, then 14 two days after.

## 5. LinkedIn, the hard edged version

His post counts are fine and his reach is small, so the levers are format and distribution.
1. **Lead with the line that costs him something.** "I arrived with $527." "I had a heart attack." These openings beat "A lot of people think...".
2. **Post the stories as a run.** Heart attack on Monday, $527 on Tuesday, the 72 unit pass on Wednesday, the seller note on Thursday, "boring investor" on Friday. Then link them in a pinned Featured post.
3. **Native video with captions**, the reels from this app, 30 to 40 seconds. Video is the format his profile has not tried.
4. **Comment first on larger operators' posts**, a real number or a real story in two sentences, within the first hour. His March spike came from a burst of profile interest. Comments on other people's posts create that without a post.
5. **Ask the question at the end that has a number in the answer.** His "what variable do investors underestimate" post type draws the most engaged comments. Ask for a number ("what is your vacancy assumption") and reply to each.
6. **Convert the base.** The ten loyal commenters are the seed for "Roast my deal" on the Circle. Invite them first.
