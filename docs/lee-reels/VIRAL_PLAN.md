## 3. Why nothing is moving, ranked by likely impact

1. **No hook in the first two seconds.** Reels are judged on early hold and watch time. A calm statement with no number and no stakes loses people before the point.
2. **Nothing worth sending.** Sends per reach is the signal most often named for reaching people who do not follow you. Philosophy lines do not get sent. A number someone can show a friend does.
3. **The page cannot convert a visitor.** No bio, no keywords in the name field, a casual selfie avatar, a highlight that says nothing. Even a good reel would lose the follow.
4. **Two reels a day with the same idea** splits a tiny audience and trains the system that the content is interchangeable.
5. **No early engagement.** The first hour decides whether a reel gets a wider test. With near zero comments there is nothing to push it.
6. **Length and voice.** Reels ran about 60 to 95 seconds with a flat cloned voice. Long, flat and abstract is the worst mix for retention.
7. **Weak distribution.** No collabs, no commenting on bigger accounts, no cross posting, hashtags that are huge and generic.

## 4. What Instagram rewards (secondhand, check against official posts)

- Three signals are named in the January 2025 commentary on Adam Mosseri's remarks, watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Sends matter most for reaching new people.
- A strong early hold is the gate. Several guides repeat that about half of viewers leave in the first three seconds and that a short reel with high retention beats a long one with low retention.
- Trial Reels need 1,000 followers, so Lee cannot use them yet. His test bed is the normal feed.
- Hashtags classify a post and do not drive reach. A few exact ones are enough. Keywords in the name, bio, caption, on screen text and spoken words are what search reads, and public professional posts are now indexed by Google.
- Reels with another app's watermark are recommended less. Upload the clean master file to every platform.
- Collab posts share the post across both accounts and are described as the strongest format for collabs.

## 5. The plan, seven levers

### Lever 1, make the page convert (one hour of work)
- **Name field** "Lee | Mobile Home Park Investing" or whatever he really does. This field is searched, the handle is not.
- **Bio** line one is who it is for and what they get ("Real deal math for new commercial investors"). Line two is the proof or the journey ("Documenting my first parks"). Line three is the keyword offer ("Comment DEAL for the park checklist").
- **Avatar** a clear face crop with good light. The current one is a casual selfie with a cap.
- **Link** one page with the free checklist, not a list of links.
- **Highlights** Start here, Deals, Math, Tools. Cover art in the reel style.
- **Pin three posts** the number reel, the deal reel, the checklist reel.
- **Following list** he follows 484 accounts. Keep it to investors, operators, brokers and lenders in his niche, since it shapes what he sees and who he talks to.

### Lever 2, one idea per reel, and it has to be a number or a story
Retire "salary versus ownership" for a month. Four pillars, rotating.
1. **Number reels** one calculation you can follow out loud ($50 of lot rent is $150,000 of value).
2. **Real deal reels** from his own market, anonymised. "A 20 lot park, $450 rent, here is what the seller wanted and what I would pay." This is the thing nobody else can copy.
3. **Myth and honest math reels** "A 10 percent cap rate is not a steal." A trade off in every reel builds trust and keeps the page safe.
4. **Journey reels** "I am trying to buy my first park. Week 3." A running series gives people a reason to follow, which a one off cannot.

The seven scripts already in `docs/lee-reels` cover pillars 1 to 3. Add pillar 4 once he has recorded his own voice.

### Lever 3, the first two seconds
- The whole hook is on screen at frame 1 and spoken in the first second. No intro, no logo, no "hey guys".
- The hook is a number, a stake or a question with a visible answer. Examples
  - A $50 decision. $150,000 of value.
  - Nobody buys a $10 million building alone.
  - The cap rate was 10 percent. The real return was 8.3.
  - Which park do you buy, A or B.
  - I almost bought this park. Here is why I didn't.
- The cover is chosen on purpose. Big number, short claim, high contrast, and his face on one reel in three.
- The closing line loops back to the opening line so a replay feels seamless, since replays add to watch time.

### Lever 4, the edit (this is what the app can do)
- **Length** 25 to 40 seconds, one idea. The existing Lee fast mode (hook at frame 1, tight pads, whip pans, counting numbers, number sounds, sentence silences cut to 0.3 seconds) is the right base.
- **A visual change every 1.5 to 2 seconds** with a number counting up, a card flipping, a bar growing, a crossed out myth. Never a still frame for more than two seconds.
- **Spoken words highlighted on screen** as they are said, since many people watch with sound off.
- **Proof on screen** a blurred deal screenshot, a calculator, a real lot photo. Flash style calculation rows are the model.
- **Face inserts** record a 5 second hook and a 3 second sign off on a phone, then cut to graphics. A face for the first and last seconds lifts trust and holds attention. Everything in the middle can stay animated.
- **Sound** the real voice if possible. Music about 20 dB under the voice, a small sound on every number and every cut.
- **Voice** if the clone stays flat, record the real voice. A flat delivery costs retention more than any design fix gains.

### Lever 5, package every reel the same way
- **First caption line** the keyword offer ("Comment DEAL and I'll send the park checklist").
- **Second and third lines** two plain sentences that repeat the hook and the main number, because captions are searched.
- **Disclaimer line** "Illustrative numbers, educational only."
- **Hashtags** three to five exact ones (#mobilehomepark #caprate #commercialrealestate), not #financialfreedom.
- **Pinned first comment** a question people can answer in one line ("Which park would you buy, A or B").
- **On screen text and spoken hook use the same words** so search and viewers both get one clear message.

### Lever 6, distribution, the part most small accounts skip
- **The first hour** post, share to Stories with a sticker, message 10 to 20 real people who actually care and ask one specific question, reply to every comment with a question.
  Do not use pods, bots or bought engagement. They break the rules and teach the system the wrong audience.
- **Comment on bigger accounts** five to ten useful comments a day on reels by investors, operators and brokers in his niche, early in the post's life. This is the cheapest way to borrow an audience.
- **Collabs** invite two or three accounts of similar size to co author a reel, each with their own real deal or number.
- **Cross post the clean master** to Facebook Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok within a day, no watermark. LinkedIn is where the buyers and lenders are, so post the same math as a short native video there.
- **The keyword funnel** comment DEAL, an automatic message sends the checklist, the checklist asks for an email or a WhatsApp number. Set it up before post one or the offer fails in public.

### Lever 7, measure and decide every week
Read these for each reel after 48 hours and keep a simple sheet.

| Number | What it tells you | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| 3 second hold or skip rate | Did the hook work | Guides say above 60 percent is strong |
| Average watch time versus length | Did the middle hold | Aim for more than half of the length |
| Sends per reach | Will strangers get it | The one to push up first |
| Non follower share of views | Is it leaving his circle | Should rise week by week |
| Keyword comments | Is the offer working | If comments come but no replies go out, fix the automation |
| Profile visits to follows | Is the page converting | Fix the bio before making more reels |

Decision rule, after seven reels keep the two formats with the best sends per reach, retire the worst, and make the next seven from the winners.

## 7. What good looks like, as goals and not predictions

Nobody can promise a viral reel, and an account with 276 followers starts from a cold audience. Realistic goals are relative.
- Within two weeks, the median reel beats his current median reach by half.
- Within four weeks, at least one reel is sent and not only liked, and a first stranger comments the keyword.
- Within eight weeks, a steady rise in non follower views and a first reel that travels beyond his circle.
Viral reels are usually a format that already works getting lucky. The job is to find the format and then make it many times.

## 8. What I would build next in the app

1. Cover generator (big number, short claim, optional face crop), exported next to every video.
2. Hook at frame 1 as the default in every style, with a second hook version exported for tests.
3. A loop ending that echoes the opening line.
4. Talking head mode. Drop in two phone clips, the app cuts them to the first and last seconds and animates the middle.
5. Proof scenes. A blurred deal screenshot with callout boxes and a number that counts up.
6. A packaging sheet for every project. Caption, first comment, three hashtags and the keyword line, written from the script.
7. One click export for Facebook Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok with no watermark.
8. A small results tracker that stores each reel's numbers and says which format to repeat.

## Risks

- Real estate and finance claims. Keep every number labelled as an example, no promised returns, and check with a lawyer or his licence rules on seller financing and lending talk.
- A cloned voice should be used only with the owner's consent, and labelling it is the safer choice. His own voice will perform better anyway.
- Never fake engagement. It is the fastest way to lose reach on a small account.
