# FlashAcq scripts - frameworks taken from guildshore

How @guildshore writes its reels, turned into fill-in frameworks for the FlashAcq page (commercial real estate
acquisitions), plus five finished scripts in `docs/flashacq-scripts/` that paste straight into **New project > Script**.

The frameworks are structures, not copy: nothing here reuses their wording, their examples or their numbers.

## What their scripts have in common

1. **One idea per reel, named in the first line.** Titles are claims or promises ("The Roll-Up Strategy", "How ... exploit ... for exponential wealth creation"), not topics.
2. **Round numbers you can do in your head.** Seven companies, $500K each, 3x, 10% down. The viewer can follow the arithmetic out loud, which is what makes it feel true.
3. **The math is walked, never dropped.** Price -> what you pay -> what you own -> what it produces -> what it is worth. Each step is one sentence with one number.
4. **The payoff is a gap between two prices for the same thing** (3x vs 6x, bought cheap vs valued high). They call it arbitrage; the viewer remembers "same thing, two prices".
5. **"You don't need to be rich"** is answered with a structure (seller financing, an SBA loan, 5-10% down), not with motivation.
6. **A twist marker** ("But here's where it gets really interesting") before the second half.
7. **Borrowed authority:** a named operator, a $-billion firm, a McKinsey-type statistic. Used in the hook or as the proof, never as decoration.
8. **A one-sentence thesis near the end** that restates the whole reel as a rule ("You don't always need a great business; you can buy great economics and build the great business").
9. **A keyword call to action, three times:** first line of the caption ("Comment HOLDCO if you want..."), the comments fill with that word, and the bio repeats it ("DM HOLDCO"). The follower action is one word, not "link in bio".
10. **Evergreen, specific, slightly contrarian.** A recession is an opportunity; a "boring" business is the wealth path; a struggling business is a badly run one.

## Six FlashAcq frameworks

Every skeleton below fits the studio's shape: a hook line, then 15-18 beats (about 55-70 s at 1.18x), then the call to action.
Numbers are real arithmetic - check each one before you post.

### 1. The Number Bomb (`01-rent-bump.txt`)
Hook: a surprising dollar figure from a tiny change.
Beats: hook -> "Not a typo / here's the math" -> the setup in round numbers -> the change -> annualise it -> the rule that explains it (value = income / cap rate) -> the result -> "same X, one change" -> the question you ask on every deal (a three-line list) -> call to action.
Best for: rent, expenses, fees, vacancy.

### 2. The Arbitrage Walk (`02-scale-premium.txt`)
Hook: "[Small things] and [big thing] don't sell at the same price. Even when they're the same."
Beats: N identical small assets -> price each at the small-deal multiple -> total -> put them under one owner (list the savings: one manager, one loan, one set of books) -> price the package at the big-deal multiple -> the gap in dollars -> its name -> "Buy small, package smart, sell big" (a three-line list).
Best for: portfolios, roll-ups, "why bigger sells for more".

### 3. Honest Math (`03-leverage-honest-math.txt`)
Hook: "[Popular number] does not mean [what people think it means]."
Beats: the deal in five numbers -> the hype version of the answer -> the real calculation -> the real (worse or different) result -> the rule that explains it -> the better question.
This is the credibility framework: guildshore sells the upside; FlashAcq can stand out by also showing when the popular move does not work.

### 4. The Affordability Unlock (`04-no-million-needed.txt`)
Hook: "You don't need [$X] to start [thing]."
Beats: "You need to understand how people actually buy" -> three structures, each one sentence and one number (small units + owner-occupied loan, seller financing, partnership) -> "These aren't shortcuts, they're structures, and they come with trade-offs" -> the first three steps (a verb list) -> call to action.
Always say the structure has a cost. It keeps the page credible and it is also what the lenders' rules require you to know.

### 5. The Walkthrough (`05-value-add-walkthrough.txt`)
Hook: "Here's how [strategy] actually creates money. Not theory. Real numbers."
Beats: buy price and income -> the work and what it costs -> what the work adds -> the new income -> the market's price for that income -> total spent vs value created -> where the gain came from (say how much is income and how much is the better cap rate) -> the three-line rule of the strategy.

### 6. Case Study and the Wave (templates - fill in with verified facts)
**Case study** (their Gores reel): `[Name] built [$ size] around one idea: [counter-intuitive thesis].` -> where they started (first deal, real numbers) -> what was wrong with the deal and what they changed (three actions, plain verbs) -> the result in months and dollars -> the thesis as a rule -> "what that means if you're buying" -> reframe line. Subjects to consider (check every fact against two sources before using): famous real estate operators who started small.
**The Wave** (their Srivatsaa reel): `[Credible person or source] says [bold claim].` -> the shift, with one statistic and its source -> why supply and demand have not met -> the different kind of path this creates -> "the next fortunes may come from [recognising X]". For FlashAcq use a current, sourced statistic (loan maturities, aging owners, rent growth) and put the source in the caption.

## Call-to-action system (their funnel, adapted)

- **One keyword per series**, short and unmistakable: e.g. `DEAL` (analysis reels), `BUYBOX` (how to start), `CAPRATE` (math reels). Put it in the **first line of the caption**, in the **last two lines of the script** and in the **bio**.
- Script ending (always two beats): `If you want [the next step], follow me.` / `I'll [show you X] here.` - the Adi / Flash styles show them as the last two clips.
- The keyword only works with an auto-reply (a DM automation tool that sends the guide when someone comments the word). That is outside this app: set it up before posting the first reel.
- Pin one comment that repeats the instruction.

## Writing for the video engine

What the story parser does with a script, so the lines you write come out as good scenes (see `script_story.py`):

- **One idea per line.** Short lines become beats; very long lines are split at a comma.
- **House rule, no colons and no semicolons** anywhere in a script. The engine also turns any that slip in into a full stop or a comma. A list needs no punctuation to start it. Three or more verb lines (`Learn the numbers.`) become numbered steps, three or more lines that open with How / What / Why / When / Where / Who become tick rows, and so do short parallel lines ("The ability to ..."). Put one normal sentence in front ("So start with what you can control.").
- **Say numbers the way you speak them:** `$48,000`, `7 percent`, `$3.75 million`, `a 40 unit building`. The scene shows the amount as a money bag and the building as a building.
- **A whole split into parts, shown as a bar.** `[stack $10 million building = $7 million lender + $3 million equity]` after the line that says it draws one bar split into its parts, each part growing in when its amount is said. Add `| show 1` to reveal only the first part and leave the rest as an open question mark. If the same beat also has a calculation in brackets, it is written under the bar.
- **Who does what becomes role cards.** Three or more sentences that start with a role ("The lender provides financing. Investors provide capital. The operator runs the property.") build a stack of cards, one per role with what they bring, each appearing at its own word. Roles known so far are sponsor, lender, investor, operator, broker, buyer, seller, developer, manager, partner, tenant and owner.
- **Three things to do stay in one beat.** "You need to bring real value, understand the risks, and earn people's trust." is kept whole and drawn as three ticked rows.
- **A question to the viewer** ("What can you bring to a deal that makes people want you involved?") shows YOU, a big question mark and the thing asked about. **"Follow me"** shows a profile and a Follow button.
- **A "not" carries over a split sentence**, so "I'm not talking about winning the lottery / or buying a piece of a REIT" crosses out both.
- **Say First, Second, Third and numbered cards build up.** A run of sentences that open with First, Second, Third (up to five) becomes a stack of cards. Each card appears at its ordinal word, the card being explained is highlighted, and the earlier ones stay on screen until the next ordinal arrives. The card label is the sentence cut down to its key words ("you can start small" becomes START SMALL).
- **Say a number and a thing, and that many appear:** "take five small apartment buildings" draws five apartment buildings, one after the other as "five" is said, with "5 APARTMENT BUILDINGS" under them (2 to 20; "a 24 unit building" stays one building; works for buildings, houses, warehouses, hotels, cars ...).
- **Show the working:** a line in square brackets is a calculation that is *shown* but not spoken, and belongs to the spoken line above it:
  `That's $4,000 a month.` then `[$100 x 40 units = $4,000 a month]`. The beat becomes a calculation scene: the sum appears first, then the answer in a mint box (with its unit) at the moment you say it. Use `x` for times, `/` for divided by, `-` for minus, `+`, `%`. Up to four calculations per beat, they stack as rows. A sum you only *speak* ("$300,000 divided by 6 percent is $5 million") is drawn automatically, without brackets. The bracket lines do not change the narration, so existing voice files stay valid.
- **Quote lines** in straight double quotes become quote cards, crossed out when they are the wrong belief ("I'll start when I have a million").
- Keep the hook to one or two short sentences: it is split in two at the hard cut.
- 145-160 words is about 55-65 seconds at speed 1.18.

## Before you post anything

- Every number re-calculated by you, with the assumption written in the caption ("illustrative example, not a forecast").
- No promise of returns, no "guaranteed", no advice to buy a specific property. Say "educational".
- Loan terms (FHA, SBA, agency, DSCR) and statistics checked against the current source, with the source named.
- If you quote a person or firm, every fact checked twice; do not describe a living person's private life.
- Do not copy a competitor's caption or script: use the structure, your own numbers and your own examples.
