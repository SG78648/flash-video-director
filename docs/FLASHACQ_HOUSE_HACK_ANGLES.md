# FlashAcq, one idea and many angles

Set 2026-10-21. This replaces the topic spread in `FLASHACQ_VIRAL_PLAN.md` section 10. The rest of that plan (account fix, rules for every reel, testing, distribution) still stands.

## The idea, which never changes

**A normal 9 to 5 worker can own a building by living in one unit and letting the tenants pay much of the loan. Little or no down.**

Guildshore does the same thing with businesses. Their idea is that a normal professional can buy a business with nothing down, and every reel is that sentence told a new way. Ours is house hacking.

Every reel has the same three fixed parts and one moving part.

| Fixed | Moving |
|---|---|
| The viewer is a regular employee, a renter or someone who thinks they cannot afford to own | The angle, meaning the hook, the proof, the character and the format |
| The payoff is that the tenants help pay your loan and you own the building | |
| The ask is the word KEYS and the guide behind it | |

Before a script is written the angle gets a one line name. If it cannot be tied back to the idea in one sentence, it is not a Flash reel.

## The angle library

Each angle is a different way into the same idea. Hooks are drafts to test, three per angle, and only the first line changes.

**1. Exposé of the other side.** The viewer pays, someone else gets the house. Done in 20 (you pay his mortgage) and 21.

**2. The cost of waiting.** What renting adds up to. Done in 22 (ten years of rent). More variants such as five years, twenty years, or "what your rent has paid so far".

**3. The down payment objection.** "I don't have $60,000." Done in 23 ($14,000 down). Variants with a veteran loan, which can allow nothing down on a building of up to four units for people who are eligible, and with first time buyer programs. Each needs its terms checked first.

**4. The myth buster.** Say the belief out loud and break it. "You don't need to be rich to own a building." "You don't have to quit your job to be a landlord." "You don't have to buy a house to live in, you can buy two homes in one."

**5. The job angle.** Aim at a specific worker so a whole group feels spoken to. Say a teacher, a nurse, a warehouse supervisor, a software tester. Use an illustrative income and run what a lender would allow. These are hypothetical people, labelled as such, and never presented as real customers.

**6. Side by side.** A renter and a house hacker with the same income over five years, one number on each side, with the loan paid down, the rent paid out and what each owns. The numbers need computing for each script and the assumptions stay on screen.

**7. The paycheck test.** What the other unit's rent does to the monthly bill, shown as the viewer's own take home. "Your tenant just gave you a raise."

**8. The news hook.** A rent report, a rate move or an affordability headline from the same week, and one number that turns it into the house hacking answer. News reels get searched and shared. Source on screen.

**9. Authority from real people.** The user wants popular names and influencers used for authority. The rule is that every claim comes from a real, checkable source that shows on screen, such as an interview, a book or a post. The script states what they said or did in our own words, never invents a quote, never implies they endorse Flash, and uses no logo or likeness that suggests it. Three ways to use it.
- A real public figure who started by house hacking or who recommends it, with the source.
- A reaction to a viral claim by a known creator, agreeing or showing the math they left out.
- A famous number such as the national median rent or the median home price, from a named agency.
These have to be researched one at a time, so none are scripted until the source is in hand.

**10. The hypothetical story.** "Say a 29 year old with a $60,000 salary..." The way scripts 20 and 21 already work. It is a story with the numbers visible, and it is always framed as an example.

**11. The comparison to something familiar.** "A duplex is a car loan where the car pays you back." Only if the numbers hold.

**12. The reply reel.** Real comments and questions on screen with the answer. The strongest angle once there are comments, since it proves a person is behind the account.

**13. Pattern interrupts.** "Your landlord hopes you never find this out." "The cheapest way to buy is the one nobody tells renters about." Only if the reel then delivers the proof.

## Format changes that are not new angles

The same angle can be told as a calculator walkthrough, a split screen, a text message conversation, a day in the life, a voiceover over a real listing, or a face on camera for ten seconds. Format is a second dial, and it is worth testing after an angle has worked.

## What I will check before any script is posted

- Every loan program (FHA, veteran, conventional) for its current terms, since the guide and the reels both lean on them.
- Every number, shown with its assumptions.
- Every claim about a real person, from the source itself.

## Trend reels, written 2026-10-23

Two news hook reels tied to what was in the news that week, both closing on the same idea.

- 28 Rates above 7 percent. Freddie Mac's weekly average was 7.32 percent on 22 October and 7.40 percent on 15 October. The reel shows what the rise does to a duplex loan and that one tenant outweighs it. Check the weekly number again on the day of posting.
- 29 The first time buyer is now 40. The National Association of Realtors 2025 profile of home buyers and sellers, with a median age of 40, a record low share of 21 percent and a median down payment of 10 percent. Put the source on screen.

Numbers in the news hook angle go stale in a week, so these two are posted soon after they are narrated or not at all.
