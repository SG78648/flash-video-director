## The hook design

Cognitive dissonance is the discomfort of holding two beliefs that do not fit. A hook creates it when it states something that contradicts a belief the viewer holds with confidence, in the viewer's own words, with a number that makes it concrete, and then the body resolves it. The viewer keeps watching to get the discomfort resolved. So each hook has three parts.

1. **A belief the viewer treats as a win.** "Full building" is success to a landlord. "Paying more" is a loss to a buyer.
2. **A flat contradiction with a number.** "100 percent full ... probably losing money." "$5,000 over the asking price ... the cheapest way."
3. **The resolution is held back until the last third**, and it is arithmetic the viewer can check, so it feels earned and not a trick.

The hook is on screen from frame 1 (the two-part headline splits at the comma, so the second half arrives as the voice says it).

## Numbers to check before posting

16
- 20 x $1,000 = $20,000. 19 x $1,100 = $20,900. 20 x $1,100 = $22,000. The break-even is 20 x ($1,000 / $1,100) = 18.18 tenants, so you can lose 1.82 tenants (almost two) and collect the same. Losing two tenants gives 18 x $1,100 = $19,800, which is less. The script says "almost two", which is right.
- A rent increase does not always keep the tenants, and a vacant unit costs more than one month of rent (cleaning, repairs, finding a tenant, see the rent raise reel). The script is about testing the price, not about raising it for everyone.

17
- $200,000 x 7 percent = $14,000. $200,000 x 3 percent = $6,000. The difference is $8,000 a year, $667 a month, and $5,000 / $667 = 7.5 months. It looks at interest only, so it ignores the principal payments and the fact that a loan usually runs for years, which makes it a simple illustration.
- 3 percent is an illustration. A seller who finances at that rate is possible but not typical, and not every state or lender lets a buyer do it the same way. The seller has to agree. Terms must be in writing and checked by a lawyer.
- A higher price can change the appraisal, the property tax assessment and the loan to value. It is not a recommendation to overpay.
