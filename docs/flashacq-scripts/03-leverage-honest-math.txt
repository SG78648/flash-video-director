An 8 percent cap rate does not mean an 8 percent return.
Here's why.
You buy a $2 million building.
It earns $160,000 a year.
That's the 8 percent cap rate.
You put down $500,000.
You borrow $1.5 million at 7 percent over 25 years.
Your loan payments come to about $127,000 a year.
[$1,500,000 at 7% for 25 years = $10,602 a month]
[$10,602 x 12 months = $127,220 a year]
That's 8.5 percent of the loan, every year.
[$127,220 / $1,500,000 = 8.5%]
The building only earns 8 percent.
[$160,000 / $2,000,000 = 8%]
So the debt takes more than it adds.
You're left with roughly $33,000 on $500,000 of cash.
[$160,000 - $127,220 = $32,780]
That's a 6.6 percent return.
[$32,780 / $500,000 = 6.6%]
Lower than the cap rate.
Leverage only helps when the property earns more than the debt costs.
Otherwise it just adds risk.
So the question isn't what's the cap rate.
It's this:
Can this building grow its income faster than my loan costs me?
